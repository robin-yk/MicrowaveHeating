Operating sequence

How to use the model

The model uses the measured microwave power absorbed by the sample and adjusts four effective heat-transfer parameters so that the calculated center and wall temperatures match the experiments. After calibration, it solves the steady two-dimensional heat balance across the powder bed, quartz tube, flowing gas, and surrounding air to estimate the full temperature map and the heat lost through gas heating, convection, and radiation. The dielectric properties determine how the measured power is distributed within the bed, while the total absorbed power remains an experimental input. The resulting temperature field is therefore a physics-based estimate that is most reliable within the calibrated material, geometry, and power range.

1 · Select a material profile

The selection loads that material's dielectric table, effective thermal parameters, skeletal density, particle diameter, and measured calibration dataset. This release contains reduced-rutile TiO₂ and as-received SiC 60–100 mesh profiles.

2 · Enter sample-absorbed power

Use microwave power absorbed inside the sample. Forward generator power minus reflected power also contains cavity-wall absorption; use that difference only after determining the sample fraction.

3 · Confirm geometry and transport inputs

Enter diameter, height, mass, gas, and flow. Bed volume is calculated automatically as πD²L/4; advanced controls contain particle diameter and gas pressure. These values determine bed void fraction, superficial velocity, pressure drop, and dimensionless flow groups.

4 · Calculate and inspect temperatures

The map shows sample, quartz, tube gas, and outside air. Read the FBG-coordinate temperature, quartz wall, sample volume average, center-to-wall difference, and gas outlet.

5 · Inspect transport and heat balance

Use Reynolds, Péclet, Nusselt, Darcy, Rayleigh, and Biot numbers to identify the controlling transport regime. Gas heating, outer convection, and radiation should sum to absorbed power.

6 · Compare with measurements

The comparison section now belongs to the 2D page. Each material retains its own calibration data; a CSV upload replaces only the selected material's dataset for the current session.

Full calculation record

Equations, parameter values, and assumptions

The sections below follow the calculation in execution order. Every power term is in watts, every internal length is in metres, and every temperature-dependent material property is evaluated from the current cell temperature. Temperatures are displayed in °C; absolute temperature is used for radiation, ideal-gas density, viscosity, conductivity ratios, and dimensionless transport groups.

Calculation sequence

Material profile, measured Pabs, and geometry → material-region map → temperature-dependent ε′, ε″, and k → normalized microwave heat source → sample/quartz/gas/air cell balances → nonlinear steady solution → transport groups, sensor temperatures, and energy balance → 2D-derived Eq. S39 reduction → material-specific experiment comparison
The Eq. S39 reduction (Calculations, section 10) receives the converged 2D average temperature and sample-boundary heat fluxes. It summarizes the spatial solution and does not feed temperatures back into the 2D solver.

1 · Inputs, cylindrical geometry, and material regions

The physical problem is axisymmetric about the tube centerline. The displayed left half is a mirror of the solved radial half-domain, so the fixed 30 × 30 mm display uses 30 × 60 annular control volumes and appears as a 60 × 60 mesh at 0.5 mm spacing.

Bed and domain dimensions

R = D/2;   Rq,o = R + tq;   Rd = Wd/2
Vbed = Vcyl = πD²L/4;   ε=1−m/(ρsVbed)
D and L determine the bed volume automatically. That volume and the entered mass set bulk density and solver void fraction; the fixed outer domain remains 30 × 30 mm.

Cell centers and dimensions

Δr = Rd/Nr;   Δz = Hd/Nz
ri = (i+½)Δr;   zj = (j+½)Δz − Hd/2
The solve uses Nr = 30 and Nz = 60 with Δr = Δz = 0.5 mm. Mirroring the radial half-domain produces the displayed 60 × 60 mesh. Indices begin at zero.

Annular cell volume and face areas

rw=iΔr;   re=(i+1)Δr
Vij=π(re²−rw²)Δz
Aw=2πrwΔz;   Ae=2πreΔz;   Az=π(re²−rw²)
These exact annular areas provide the 1/r term in cylindrical heat conduction without a singularity at r = 0. The west-face area is zero on the symmetry axis.

Material assignment

Selected sample: r<R and |z|<L/2
Tube gas: r<R and |z|≥L/2
Quartz: R≤r<Rq,o;   Static air: r≥Rq,o
The quartz tube runs through the full domain height. Gas occupies the tube above and below the centered powder bed. Static air occupies the region between the quartz outer wall and the fixed outer 30 × 30 mm boundary.

2 · Temperature-dependent material properties

Complex Looyenga dielectric mixing

(ε*eff)1/3=(1−ε)(ε*s)1/3+ε(ε*g)1/3
The embedded packed-bed dielectric table is first unmixed at its reference void fraction, then remixed at the current mass and D/L-derived volume void fraction. Manual override uses the table directly.

Effective sample-bed conductivity

keff=ksk[2ksk+kg−2ε(ksk−kg)]/[2ksk+kg+ε(ksk−kg)]
kz=χkkeff,r
An equivalent contact skeleton conductivity ksk is recovered from each calibrated reference-bed k anchor. Current porosity and gas conductivity then set keff. Manual override uses the reference anchors directly.

Tube-gas conductivity

kHe(T)=0.1513(TK/293.15)0.72
kN₂(T)=0.0258(TK/293.15)0.72
Conductivity is in W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. These power laws provide the temperature dependence used in tube-gas cells.

Static-air and quartz conductivity

kair,eff(T)=Fair·0.0263(TK/293.15)0.76
kquartz= entered constant
Fair = 3.0 by default. It increases diffusive air heat transport to represent unresolved natural circulation inside the 30 × 30 mm region. The model does not calculate an air velocity field.

3 · Microwave penetration and volumetric heat generation

Free-space wave number and power penetration depth

k0=2πf/c0
α=k0{[√(ε′²+ε″²)−ε′]/2}1/2;   Dp=1/(2α)
c0 = 299,792,458 m s⁻¹. Dp is the 1/e penetration distance for microwave power. Each sample cell uses its profile's ε′(Tij) and ε″(Tij).

Centered TE111 field-shape prior

F(r,z)=exp{−[r/(wrR)]²−[z/(wzL/2)]²}
wr and wz are the two editable Gaussian-width ratios. This analytic profile places the electric-field maximum at the sample center. It replaces a measured or Maxwell-solved TE111 electric-field map.

Penetration weighting

A(r,z,T)=exp[−(R−r)/Dp(T)]
The factor is one at the radial bed surface and decreases toward the center. Center heating therefore requires the Gaussian cavity-field maximum and thermal insulation to outweigh radial attenuation.

Unnormalized cell absorption weight

wij=ε″(Tij)F(ri,zj)A(ri,zj,Tij)
Only selected-sample cells receive a microwave source. Quartz, tube gas, and outside-air cells receive no direct microwave heating in this implementation.

Exact normalization to measured sample-absorbed power

QMW,ij=Pabs[wijVij/Σbed(wV)];   q‴MW,ij=QMW,ij/Vij
ΣbedQMW,ij=Pabs
The entered ε′ and ε″ change the spatial distribution through Dp and wij. They do not calculate the total absorbed power because Pabs is supplied directly. Forward power and reflected power require a separate cavity/sample power partition before use here.

4 · Heat conduction and bed–quartz contact

Continuous steady equations

Sample: 0=(1/r)∂/∂r(krr∂T/∂r)+∂/∂z(kz∂T/∂z)+q‴MW−q‴gas
Other regions: 0=∇·(k∇T)
The sample equation permits different radial and axial conductivities. Quartz, tube gas, and outside air use isotropic conductivity.

Two-cell face conductance

Gij=[δ/(2kiA)+δ/(2kjA)+Rc]−1
Rc=1/(hcontactA) at sample–quartz; otherwise Rc=0
δ is Δr for radial faces and Δz for axial faces. This harmonic-resistance form gives one identical heat rate on both sides of a material interface.

Cell heat balance

0=QMW,i+ΣneighborsGij(Tj−Ti)−Ggas,i(Ti−Tg,in)−Grad,i(Ti−Ta)
The gas term exists only in sample cells. The radiation term exists only on quartz cells directly adjacent to outside air. Boundary convection is added on the outer radial, top, and bottom faces.

Axis and external boundaries

r=0: ∂T/∂r=0
r=Rd, z=±Hd/2: −kair,eff∂T/∂n=hout(T−Ta)
Automatic mode recalculates hout with Churchill–Chu from the current boundary temperature during nonlinear iteration. Manual override uses the entered coefficient.

5 · Flowing-gas sensible heating

Flow rate and heat-capacity rate

ṅ=Qsccm·10⁻⁶/(60·0.022414)
Cg=ṅCp,m
The standard molar volume is 0.022414 m³ mol⁻¹. Cp,m = 20.786 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ for He and 29.124 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ for N₂. Cg has units W K⁻¹.

Wakao gas–solid conductance

Nup=2+1.1Rep0.6Pr1/3;   hgs=Nupkg/dp
as=6(1−ε)/dp;   UAgs=hgsasVbed
Automatic mode derives the overall and per-slice effectiveness from UAgs/Cg. Manual override converts the entered overall effectiveness back to UA.

Axial gas-temperature march

Tg,j+1=Tg,j+ηj(T̄bed,j−Tg,j)
T̄bed,j=ΣiVijTij/ΣiVij
The inlet begins at ambient temperature. Each bed slice transfers heat to the gas before the next axial slice is evaluated.

Gas sink distributed across a bed slice

Ggas,ij=CgηjVij/Vslice,j
Qgas=Cg(Tg,out−Ta)
The slice heat-transfer conductance is distributed in proportion to annular cell volume. The final outlet temperature supplies the gas term in the reported energy balance.

6 · Quartz radiation and outside-air heat rejection

Quartz radiation to the cooled cavity wall

Qrad,i=εqFAσAi(Ti,K⁴−Ta,K⁴)
hrad,i=εqFAσ(Ti,K+Ta,K)(Ti,K²+Ta,K²)
σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴. Radiation is applied on the quartz outer surface and uses the entered cavity-wall temperature. FA is an effective radiating-area/view-factor multiplier.

Conduction through the static-air region

0=(1/r)∂/∂r(kair,effr∂T/∂r)+∂/∂z(kair,eff∂T/∂z)
The external air temperature field is part of the finite-volume solution. The model resolves its temperature gradient while Fair represents circulation that a conduction-only equation cannot generate.

Outer-domain natural convection

Qboundary=Σouter faceshCC(T)A(Tcell−Ta)
Churchill–Chu hCC is recalculated from the current wall/ambient film temperature and applied to the radial, top, and bottom boundaries. Selecting Manual override freezes h at the entered value.

Heat-flow path represented

Sample bed → quartz/tube gas → surrounding air and cavity-wall radiation → 20 °C boundary
Quartz conduction, gas conduction, external-air conduction, gas sensible heating, outer convection, and radiation are simultaneous. There is no separate fitted “support loss” term.

7 · Dimensionless transport groups

Gas state, velocity, and viscosity

ρg=pM/(RgT);   Qactual=ṅRgT/p;   us=Qactual/(πR²)
μ(T)=μ0(T/T0)3/2(T0+S)/(T+S)
The gas-property temperature is the mean of inlet and calculated outlet temperature. T0 = 293.15 K. He uses M = 0.004002602 kg mol⁻¹, μ0 = 1.96×10⁻⁵ Pa·s, S = 79.4 K; N₂ uses M = 0.0280134 kg mol⁻¹, μ0 = 1.76×10⁻⁵ Pa·s, S = 111 K.

Tube-flow groups

ReD=ρusD/μ;   Pr=μCp/k;   PeD=ReDPr
Gz=ReDPrD/L
NuD=3.66+0.0668Gz/[1+0.04Gz2/3]
ReD classifies empty-tube flow, Pr compares momentum and thermal diffusivity, PeD compares advection with thermal diffusion, and Gz measures thermal entrance development. hD = NuDkg/D is displayed with the table.

Bed void fraction and permeability

ρbulk=m/Vbed;   ε=1−ρbulk/ρsolid
K=ε³dp²/[150(1−ε)²];   Da=K/R²;   (dP/dz)viscous=μus/K
Skeletal density belongs to the selected material profile. The Blake–Kozeny permeability uses the entered mean particle diameter and the coefficient 150, which is the same constant the Ergun viscous term uses, so μus/K reproduces that term identically. The coefficient was previously 180 against Ergun's 150, which made Darcy's law and the reported ΔP describe beds differing by exactly 20%.

Packed-bed Reynolds, Péclet, and Nusselt numbers

Rep=ρusdp/μ;   Rep,int=Rep/ε;   Pep=RepPr
Nup=2+1.1Rep0.6Pr1/3;   hgs=Nupkg/dp
The Wakao expression estimates particle-to-gas heat transfer. The page labels low-Re values as an extrapolation because the present sccm flow lies below its main fitted range.

Ergun pressure drop, Euler, and Archimedes numbers

ΔP/L=150μ(1−ε)²us/(ε³dp²)+1.75ρ(1−ε)us²/(ε³dp)
Eu=ΔP/(ρus²);   Ar=gdp³ρ(ρsolid−ρ)/μ²
Eu nondimensionalizes the Ergun pressure loss. Ar compares particle buoyancy/gravity with viscous stress and helps screen particle-motion or fluidization relevance.

Outside-air natural convection

GrL=gβΔTL³/ν²;   RaL=GrLPrair
NuL={0.825+0.387RaL1/6/[1+(0.492/Prair)9/16]8/27}²
Properties use the quartz-wall/ambient film temperature. Air uses M = 0.0289652 kg mol⁻¹, Cp = 1007 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, μ0 = 1.81×10⁻⁵ Pa·s, S = 111 K, and molecular kair before Fair is applied. The Churchill–Chu vertical-surface result gives hnatural = NuLkair/L.

Bed-scale Biot numbers

Bicontact=hcontactR/kbed;   Birad=hradR/kbed
The converged volume-average bed temperature sets kbed. Values above roughly 0.1 indicate that internal bed conduction can sustain a measurable temperature gradient relative to the corresponding boundary transfer rate.

Microwave penetration ratio

Πpenetration=Dp/R
This electromagnetic dimensionless ratio accompanies the transport groups. Dp/R above one indicates that power attenuates by less than one e-fold over one bed radius.

8 · Nonlinear steady-state solution

Local algebraic update

Ti*=[QMW,i+ΣGijTj+Ggas,iTg,in+Grad,iTa]/[ΣGij+Ggas,i+Grad,i]
Boundary conductances and their ambient-temperature terms are included in the numerator and denominator on outer cells.

Successive over-relaxation

Tin+1=Tin+ω(Ti*−Tin)
The standard calculation uses ω = 1.05 and two full cell sweeps per nonlinear iteration. Temperatures are bounded from ambient to 2500 °C to prevent an unphysical numerical step.

Property/source feedback loop

T → ε′, ε″, k, Dp, q‴MW, hrad, Tgas → new T
Every nonlinear iteration rebuilds the normalized microwave source, gas-temperature march, temperature-dependent conductivities, and radiation coefficient.

Initial field and convergence rule

T0=Ta+22Pabsexp(−d/0.006)
max|Tn+1−Tn|<3×10⁻⁴ °C;   n≤6000
d is distance from the bed envelope. The initial field only accelerates iteration. A converged steady result is independent of this starting guess when the nonlinear equations have one reachable stable root.

9 · Reported temperatures and energy-conservation test

Temperature outputs

T̄bed=ΣbedVijTij/ΣbedVij
The FBG value is the cell containing the entered (r,z) sensor coordinate. Center is the r = 0, z = 0 cell. Quartz-wall temperature is the first quartz cell outside r = R at z = 0. Maximum and minimum values are searched only inside sample cells.

Global steady-state balance

Pabs=Qgas+Qboundary+Qrad+Enum
Residual (%)=100|Enum|/max(Pabs,10⁻³⁰)
The page marks a run acceptable when the cell iteration converges and the global power residual is below 0.5%.

10 · 2D-derived average-temperature reduction

Published reduced-order heat balance (Supporting Information Eq. S39)

0=−2CgeomLks[T̄s−(T̄s+BiT∞)/(1+Bi)]−Gface(T̄s−T∞)+Pabs
Gtube=Qsample→quartz/(T̄2D−T∞);   Bi=Gtube/(2πLks−Gtube)
Gface=(Qaxial+Qgas)/(T̄2D−T∞);   T̄s=T∞+Pabs/(Gtube+Gface)
The solver evaluates ks at the 2D volume-average temperature, extracts radial and axial sample-boundary heat flows from the converged cell-face conductances, and includes gas sensible heat in the equivalent face path. Cgeom is fixed at π. The resulting one-temperature model is an interpretable reduction of the 2D solution, not an independent prediction.

11 · Material-specific experimental calibration

Material-profile loading rule

profile = {name, ρsolid, dp, ε′(T), ε″(T), kr(T), kz/kr, heat-transfer defaults, measured [Pabs, Twall, Tcenter] rows}
Changing the material selector replaces every listed field as one profile. An uploaded calibration CSV replaces the measured rows attached to the active material for the current browser session. The bundled TiO₂ and SiC profiles use the same solver and keep their dielectric and calibration datasets separate.

Residual and root-mean-square error

ek=Tcalculated,k−Tmeasured,k
RMSE=[Σek²/N]1/2
Center and quartz-wall RMSE are calculated separately over powered rows only. Combined RMSE pools both residual sets. The zero-power row appears on the graph and table but is omitted from RMSE.

Bounded coordinate-search calibration

min RMSEcombined[kr,200,kr,500,kr,800,FA]
The search tests positive and negative steps for one parameter at a time on a 10 × 30 coarse mesh, using step fractions 0.20 and 0.08 of each bound. Final metrics are recomputed on the selected display mesh. This deterministic coordinate search does not prove a unique optimum.

Calibration bounds

0.20≤kr,200≤3.0;   0.15≤kr,500≤2.0
0.10≤kr,800≤1.5 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹;   0.30≤FA≤3.0
All other parameters remain fixed during automatic calibration.

13 · Supplementary Note 3 compatibility audit

The present browser solver is a steady thermal surrogate inspired by a volume-averaged porous-continuum model; it is not a full implementation of the coupled electromagnetic, Darcy-flow, energy, and species equations in Supplementary Note 3. The table distinguishes equations actually solved from closures used only to prescribe a source or report a diagnostic.

14 · Recommended path toward Supplementary Note 3

1 · Make the source electromagnetic

Solve the axisymmetric or full-3D frequency-domain Maxwell problem for complex tensor ε* and μ=μ0, enforce tangential field continuity, and compute q‴MW=½ωε0ε″r,eff|E|². Use port power and cavity losses so Pabs becomes an output; retain the present normalized source only as an explicitly labeled measured-power mode.

2 · Add averaged flow (done, uncoupled)

Continuity plus Darcy is now solved with a longitudinal/transverse Keff tensor and temperature-dependent ideal-gas density, at the measured inlet flow with the outlet at the entered pressure. Measurement retracts two earlier recommendations here: Rep≈3×10⁻³–2×10⁻², so the Ergun inertial term is under 0.1% of ΔP and Forchheimer is not worth adding; and ΔP/P<0.2%, so the ρ∝P nonlinearity is negligible and the pressure equation stays linear. What remains is feeding the face mass fluxes into the energy equation.

3 · Choose one thermal closure

Local thermal equilibrium is the right choice here, and measurement rather than assumption settles it: UAgs/Cg ≈ 3×10⁴–3×10⁵, so the automatic closure already runs at ηgs = 1.0000 and the gas reaches bed temperature almost immediately. Solve one LTE energy equation with (ρCp)eff and K*=Keff+Kh, where Kh is numerically negligible because Pep ≈ 2×10⁻³–1×10⁻². An LTNE extension would be defensible only at far higher flow.

4 · Add chemistry conservatively

Solve averaged advection–dispersion for every species. Convert catalytic surface rates into a macroscale source using specific interfacial area, retain homogeneous rates separately, and add reaction enthalpy consistently to the selected energy closure.

5 · Determine tensors and validity

Obtain ε* and Keff from unit-cell homogenization or measurements, and K* and D*i from validated correlations or closure problems. Record lc/Lc and lc/λ for every geometry rather than assuming scale separation silently.

6 · Verify in stages

First reproduce the current heat balance with a prescribed source, then verify Maxwell power conservation, Darcy pressure drop, mesh independence, LTE/LTNE sensitivity, and species conservation. Validate temperatures, reflected power, pressure drop, outlet composition, and uncertainty against independent experiments.

15 · Physical scope and limitations

Directly resolved

Radial and vertical temperatures in sample, quartz, tube gas, and outside air; porosity-adjusted dielectric and thermal properties; Wakao gas transfer; Ergun pressure correction; contact resistance; quartz radiation; and Churchill–Chu boundary convection.

Calculated diagnostics

Reynolds, Prandtl, Péclet, Graetz, Nusselt, Darcy, Euler, Archimedes, Grashof, Rayleigh, and Biot numbers; permeability; specific gas–solid area; UA; effective volumetric heat capacity; Ergun pressure drop; and the Darcy pressure and superficial-velocity field with its flow-maldistribution ratio.

Absent from the temperature solver

Maxwell field solution, resonance detuning, cavity-wall microwave absorption, Navier–Stokes momentum, advective coupling of the solved Darcy field back into the energy balance, particle-scale hot spots, transient heat capacity, species and reaction equations, temperature-dependent quartz properties, thermometry bias, and prediction intervals for a new experiment.

Measured regime, so the next stage need not re-derive it (both embedded profiles at their default settings): the gas carries only 1.1–1.5% of absorbed power, outer convection 12–27%, and quartz radiation 71–87%; that dominant radiation channel is fitted through the empirical radiating-area multiplier, not derived. Coupling the Darcy field into the energy equation therefore changes a small channel; the largest remaining physics gap is the radiation closure, not the flow. Supporting numbers: Rep ≈ 3×10⁻³–2×10⁻², Pep ≈ 2×10⁻³–1×10⁻², UAgs/Cg ≈ 3×10⁴–3×10⁵, ΔP ≈ 27–170 Pa (under 0.2% of inlet pressure), axial advection Péclet 0.01–0.25.
